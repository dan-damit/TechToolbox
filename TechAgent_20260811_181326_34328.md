## Yes, fundamentally it is massive amounts of arithmetic.

When you look under the hood of a neural network (the type of AI model used in large language models), every single step involves linear algebra. Specifically, matrix multiplication and addition.

Here is how that math works during iteration:

### 1. The Core Operation: Matrix Multiplication
At its heart, an AI layer takes input data (numbers representing words or pixels) and multiplies them by a **weight matrix**. 
- Input vector x Weight matrix = Output vector.
This operation calculates the weighted sum of inputs to produce a result.

### 2. Iteration During Training (Learning)
When you asked if data is retained, it helps to understand that during training, the model iterates through this math repeatedly:
- **Forward Pass**: The model does the matrix multiplication and adds a small number called a 'bias'. It produces an answer.
- **Loss Calculation**: The system compares its mathematical output to the correct answer. This is just subtraction (Error = Predicted - Actual).
- **Backward Pass (Gradient Descent)**: Using calculus, it calculates how much each weight contributed to the error. 
- **Update Step**: It adjusts the weights slightly in the opposite direction of the error.

This process repeats millions or billions of times. The 'learning' is simply the model finding a specific set of numbers (weights) that minimizes the math error across all examples it has seen.

### 3. Iteration During Inference (Usage)
When you use an AI, it stops learning and only does one type of iteration:
- **Forward Pass**: It takes your prompt, converts words to numbers (embeddings), runs them through thousands of layers of matrix multiplication, applies a mathematical function called 'softmax' to turn the result into probabilities, and picks the highest probability as its next word.

### Summary on Data Retention
To answer your previous question: The raw data is **not** retained in the parameters. 
- If you train an AI on 100 photos of a cat, it does not store those 100 images inside its weights.
- Instead, it compresses the *patterns* found in those images into mathematical relationships (weights). It learns that 'whiskers + ears + fur texture' mathematically correlates to the concept of 'cat'.

So yes, you are essentially watching a computer do trillions of multiplications per second to find patterns and minimize error.
